\chapter*{Abstract}
\markboth{Abstract}{Abstract}
Delaying computing jobs until electricity is cleaner may reduce carbon emissions, but also changes competition for shared resources. This dissertation evaluates that trade-off using Stanage job records and trace-driven Slurm simulation. Seven admission policies are compared on five matched dates through 55 completed replays. Carbon estimates combine simulated occupancy, regional carbon intensity and an explicit power scenario.

History-only achieves a mean daily reduction of 0.1021\% in the primary load-dependent estimate, with 4.97 minutes of additional reported total waiting per evaluation job. Other results vary by date, and no policy meets every frozen stability condition. Queue timing, model assumptions and concentrated user delay limit the interpretation of small savings. A separate feedback prototype tests delay safeguards. The study provides a completed evaluation of these trade-offs, not measured Stanage electricity savings.
